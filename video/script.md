## Narration

### Welcome

**cold_open** · on screen: cover

Laos, spring 1975. Saigon has fallen, the Americans are leaving, and the people who fought beside them have nowhere to go. This is Chao Fa, a cooperative board game based on real-life stories, and this video will teach you how to play it.

**menu** · on screen: menu

We will look at the goal of the game, the pieces in the box, how to set it up, what a season of play looks like, the actions, fights, the story cloth, Winter, the river crossing, and how the game ends. Then we will play through a season together. The chapter markers on screen let you jump to any part.

### The game and its goal

**ov_story** · on screen: scenes/01_leaving_the_village_1200.jpg

You are Hmong. You are a countryless nation: a people with no country of your own, and after this spring, not even the hills you were born in. Your village secretly supported the United States and its CIA-backed army. Now the communists hold the capital, and the family must flee into the jungle.

**ov_goal** · on screen: scenes/05_crossing_the_mekong_1200.jpg

The goal is simple to say and hard to do. Walk south through the hills, the jungle and the lowlands. Recover the nine squares of a story cloth, each one won with a key: a contact, a route, a signal. Then cross the Mekong River into Thailand. You have five years: twenty seasons, from spring 1975 to the winter of 1979.

**ov_coop** · on screen: party

Chao Fa is fully cooperative, for one to six players, and a game takes ninety minutes to three hours. Everyone at the table plays one villager of the same family. You win together when at least one person reaches the Thai bank, and you lose together when no one does. It is a hard game by design. Play it like the people it is about: carefully, together, and without giving up.

### What is in the box

**comp_villagers** · on screen: villager_card, portraits_sheet.jpg

Let's look at what you play with. Sixteen villager cards: nine men and boys, seven women and girls. Each has three numbers. HP is how many wounds they can take, ATK is what they add to the family's attack, and DEF to its defence. Every villager has six points in all, divided differently. Side A gives a power used once a season. Side B gives a trait that is always on. You pick a side at setup and keep it for the whole game.

**comp_tiles** · on screen: map

Sixteen place tiles make the map: the home village, then four rows of three places, the hills, the jungle, the lowlands and the river. Each tile shows its danger number, the grade of fight you meet there, and what can be foraged in each season. Every place has its own search deck, with that place's picture on the back: the items, food, shelter, clues, keys and dangers you would really find there.

**comp_decks** · on screen: season_card, stranger

Then the shared decks. Eighty-five Season cards: patrols, ambushes, fog, food, omens, history and moral dilemmas. One is drawn at the start of every season. Stranger cards, people you meet on the trail, each a choice of two. Six Turning Point cards, six Law cards, and twenty-one River cards for the crossing.

**comp_fortune** · on screen: fortune

And the Fortune deck. Twenty cards numbered one to twenty. There are no dice in Chao Fa. Whenever the game needs a number, you draw the top card of the Fortune deck. We will come back to it.

**comp_board** · on screen: web/Board_assembled.png, cloth

The board holds the map and its search decks on the left, and on the right the time track, Food, Hope, the story cloth and the phase track. Along the bottom runs the Mekong crossing lane. There is a standee for every villager, a party pawn, tokens and markers, a fight tracker, the two-faced Patrol table card, a family board for every seat, and the nine squares of the story cloth.

### Setting up

**setup_map** · on screen: map_start

Setting up. Put the Village Ruins at the top of the map, face up, and the party pawn on it. For each row below it, shuffle that row's four tiles, lay three of them face down, and put the fourth back in the box. The river row has only three tiles. Shuffle each place's search deck and set it beside its tile. Or use a setup card, which lays the tiles in a historical order.

**setup_party** · on screen: setup

Now the family. The party is always three to six villagers. One player runs three, two players run two each, and three to six players take one each. Each player chooses side A or B. Every villager starts with one item: the first of their own items, the one thing they carried out of the village. Take it out of its search deck and put it on the family board. A good first party is Chai, Mai, Chao and Blong.

**setup_tracks** · on screen: rail, setup_numbers

Set Food to three times the number of villagers: a party of four starts with twelve. Set Hope to three, and the time marker on season one, Spring 1975. A small family starts with the first squares of the cloth already sewn: a party of six starts with squares one and two, five with one to three, four with one to four, and three with one to five. The rest you must win.

**setup_decks** · on screen: setup_decks

Shuffle the Fortune deck. Shuffle the Season deck in two stacks: the cards marked From 1977 go under the rest, so the war arrives in its own time. Shuffle the Laws, the Strangers, the Turning Points and the River deck, and set its three monsoon cards aside. Keep the Ending and Voice cards for the end of the game. You are ready to play.

### Playing a season

**season_overview** · on screen: phasebar, season_steps

A game is up to twenty seasons, and one season is one round of the table, in three steps. First, draw a Season card and resolve it. Second, each villager takes two actions. Third, the end of the season: sick people nobody nursed draw for fever, and in Winter everyone eats and faces the Toll. Then the time marker moves forward one season. Time only moves forward.

**season_card** · on screen: season_card

The Season card first. A patrol card is a fight wherever you are. Food goes to the store. A fog or shelter card goes to a player's hand to use later. A Time card costs you time. A dilemma must be answered by the whole party before anyone acts, and an omen stays face up, moving you toward the Turning Point.

**season_actions** · on screen: turnbox

Then the actions. Each villager takes two, or one if they are sick. If Hope is at zero, everyone has one action less. Go around the table; a villager may take both actions at once or split them. Travel is the exception: the whole party moves together, and only before anyone has acted that season.

**season_food** · on screen: food

One rule shapes the whole game: hard work eats. A Search, a Scout, building a camp, and a journey each cost one Food from the store. With no Food left, the worker still goes, but takes a wound. Outside Winter, nobody eats at the end of the season. You eat by working, so every action is a choice about food.

### The actions

**act_search** · on screen: found_item

The actions. Search is the heart of the game. Pay one Food and draw the top card of this place's search deck. An item goes to anyone in the party; a villager's own item goes to them. Food goes to the store. Shelter and remedy cards go to your hand. A Danger card is a fight, right now. A key starts a key encounter.

**act_noise** · on screen: noise

Searching the same place again makes noise. Before the second and every later search, draw a Fortune card: on the number of earlier searches here or less, soldiers come to look. You fight at the place's level, with no spoils, and then you search. Cover your tracks once a season, and the place counts two fewer searches.

**act_scout** · on screen: scout

Scout, one Food. Turn face up one hidden tile next to the party: ahead, diagonally ahead, or to the side. Now you know its danger, its grade of fight and what grows there, and you may Travel there in a later season.

**act_travel** · on screen: travel

Travel, one Food and the whole season. The whole party moves to a face-up tile next to it. Draw a Fortune card against the tile's danger number: on that number or less, a patrol finds you and you fight as an ambush. A Guide makes the draw three better; Lao clothes or a travel pass, two better. You gather the tile's forage on the way, and the season ends when you arrive.

**act_forage** · on screen: forage

Forage is free. Gain the Food shown on the tile for this season, plus one. Draw a Fortune card: on one to three you are spotted, and you fight as an ambush.

**act_rest** · on screen: rest

Rest or nurse, also free. Rest heals one wound, one more at a finished camp. Or nurse a sick villager: with medicine in the party the fever breaks on a Fortune card of six or more, otherwise on eleven or more. With Kong on side B in the party, or Lee or Pa nursing, it always breaks.

**act_camp** · on screen: camp

Build a camp, one Food per action. Two actions finish a hidden camp on the tile. It makes the Winter Toll one better and resting heal one more, for as long as you stay. Powers, playing a card from your hand, and giving an item to someone else are actions too, unless the card says they are free.

**act_wait** · on screen: wait_actions

When the party cannot move, there is still work. Lay an ambush, once at each place, and the next fight there the party fires first. Trade an item for three Food where people live. Share a meal: two Food for one Hope. Or seek help, once a season, and draw a Stranger card: a trader, a deserter, a shaman, lost children. Each is a choice of two, and the party decides together.

**act_carry** · on screen: villager_items

A word on carrying. Each villager has two hands, one body, one head and three pack slots. A rifle or a bow takes both hands; a knife takes one. Animals, boats and food caches belong to the whole party and take no slot. When someone has too much, hand an item to someone with room, or leave it in a cache on the tile.

### The Fortune deck

**fortune** · on screen: fortune_rule

Back to the Fortune deck, because everything else runs on it. Twenty cards, one to twenty, shuffled face down. Whenever a rule asks for a draw, turn the top card, read the number against the rule, and put the card on the discard. When the deck runs out, shuffle the discard and go on. Because every number comes out once before any comes again, a run of bad cards means better ones are coming, and a table that counts can feel the deck turning. Each card also carries a Hmong saying for the moment.

### Fights

**fight_when** · on screen: scenes/02_hiding_in_the_jungle_1200.jpg

Fights. A fight happens when you draw a Danger or patrol card, when a forager is spotted, when you are found travelling, at a key, and at the river. A fight has three steps: read the soldiers' number, clash up to three times, and count the cost. There is nothing to add or multiply.

**fight_step1** · on screen: patrol_table

Step one: read the soldiers' number. The soldiers have a single number, and it is their attack, their defence and their hit points all at once. Find it on the Patrol table. The row is the grade of the fight: Easy, Medium, Hard, Deadly, or the River Patrol. The column is how many of you are living. The block is the year: 1975 and 76, or 1977 onward, when the hunt doubles. A party of four meeting a Hard patrol in 1977 faces fourteen.

**fight_party** · on screen: fight_header

Your side adds together the HP, ATK and DEF of every living villager, their items, and any cards played for this fight. Lay the fight tracker between you and write the numbers in.

**fight_step2** · on screen: clash

Step two: clash, up to three times. Each clash, first draw a Fortune card. On one to four the soldiers' aim is true: plus two attack for them this clash. On seventeen to twenty the villagers find their mark: plus two for you. Then both sides strike at once. You deal your ATK minus the soldiers' number. They deal their number minus your DEF, and always at least one, because gunfire gets through. The party chooses who takes the wounds, and Armor stops them first.

**fight_example** · on screen: fight

Here is a fight in the app. The soldiers fall when the damage you have dealt reaches their number. After three clashes, if they still stand, the party breaks away with its wounds and nothing gained. A fallen villager's ATK and DEF leave the party at once. Draw a Fortune card for them: on one to ten they were killed, on eleven to twenty they were taken prisoner. Either way they leave the game.

**fight_mods** · on screen: fight_mods

A few things change a fight. In an ambush the soldiers fire first: you deal nothing in the first clash. If you laid an ambush yourself, it is the other way round. An escape card, like a Hidden Path or Fog in the Valley, lets you slip away with no fight at all. After the first or second clash you may break away: drop one item per villager and take no hit. An Easy fight is village militia, and you may hand over two Food or a money item instead of fighting. And a loud weapon leaves an Alarm token: the next fight there this season is one row harder.

**fight_step3** · on screen: spoils

Step three: count the cost. Win, and the party searches this place in safety: one card for an Easy fight, two for Medium, three for Hard, skipping Danger, Nothing and key cards. The damage a survivor took becomes wounds. A fight you only survived gives nothing.

### The story cloth and its keys

**cloth_what** · on screen: cloth

The way out is a story cloth of nine squares, laid out three by three. Story cloths were first stitched in the Thai refugee camps by families who had made this crossing, to record the war and the flight. Each square shows one stage of the escape. When a square is recovered, read its story aloud from the Companion. You need all nine, whatever the size of your party.

**cloth_keys** · on screen: key_card

The squares you did not start with are won with keys hidden in the search decks: a letter from Nong Khai, signal fires on the ridge, a patrol timetable, a fisherman's signal. Keys are scarce near home and plentiful near the river. Each key you win recovers the next missing square, and the living villager carrying the fewest squares takes it.

**cloth_encounter** · on screen: key_encounter, key_kinds

No square comes free. Each key names what stands between you and it, and the fight is always one step harder than the place: Easy becomes Medium, Medium becomes Hard, Hard becomes Deadly. A Guarded key is a fight. A Price key is paid with silver, baht, coins or two Food, or taken by force. A key On the Fallen is taken now, and then his comrades ambush you. A Mined key costs everyone a Fortune draw, with wounds on one to four. Wait for Dark costs a Food each, and a patrol finds you on one to eight. Or leave the key for now: put it at the bottom of the deck with a Guard token on the tile, and come back for it.

**cloth_fallen** · on screen: square_won

If a villager carrying squares is killed by soldiers or taken prisoner, their squares lie where they fell: shuffle each into the top three cards of that place's deck, to be won again. A villager who dies among the family, of hunger, sickness or the cold, hands their squares on. And clues help you along the way: a Guide makes every travel draw three better, a Trail Map reveals the next row, and a Safe Crossing Place or a Boatman stays with its tile for the crossing.

### Winter, Hope and the council

**winter_meal** · on screen: scenes/04_winter_in_the_hills_1200.jpg

Winter. Every fourth season, at the end of the season, everyone eats one Food. Each Food you are short is a wound to someone who has not gone hungry yet, and that person stays hungry until the next meal. Each Spring, everyone who ate heals one wound.

**winter_toll** · on screen: toll

Then the Toll. The cold gets worse every year. Every villager draws a Fortune card and falls sick on five or less, adjusted: plus one for a child, an elder, the wounded or the already sick; plus one for each Food short this year; plus the cold of the year, from zero in 1975 to two in 1978 and 79; worse at low Hope and better at high Hope; one better with a healer or a finished camp; and better for anyone holding a blanket, a poncho, a mosquito net or quinine. A villager who is already sick and fails the Toll dies.

**winter_sick** · on screen: toll_result

Sick villagers act once a season instead of twice. They must be nursed each season, or at the end of it they draw for fever, and die on a Fortune card of one to five. Nurse them early.

**winter_hope** · on screen: hope

Hope runs from zero to six and starts at three. Each death costs one Hope, unless a funeral is held. A hungry Winter costs one. Dilemmas and strangers raise or lower it, and sharing a meal raises it. At Hope zero everyone has one action less and the Toll is two worse. At Hope five or six the Toll is one better. And at the river, Hope buys a second chance.

**winter_council** · on screen: council

After the Toll, the families hold a council. Draw two Law cards the party has not adopted. The party may adopt one and shuffles the other back. Council of Elders, Travel by Night, Proper Funerals, Everyone Nurses the Sick, Strict Rations, Travel Light. Laws cannot be undone, and every one has a cost. Then the time marker moves to the next Spring.

### Crossing the Mekong

**river_when** · on screen: cross_button

The river. With all nine squares on the cloth, the party may Cross as an action from any river place. A Safe Crossing Place on that tile helps every check, and a Boatman there sells seats for silver. The crossing has three parts: the River Patrol on the bank, the boats and rafts, and the water.

**river_patrol** · on screen: river_patrol

First, the River Patrol: Lao and Vietnamese soldiers with a machine gun and a boat. Read the River Patrol row of the Patrol table; it counts at most five people. Escape cards and ambushes do not apply here. Fight up to three clashes. Beat them and you reach the water unseen. If they still stand, the party breaks for the water under fire: the Boatman will not come, but a canoe you found can still be launched.

**river_boats** · on screen: boats

Second, boats and rafts. A dugout canoe carries two. With a Boatman, each money item buys one seat, and the boat takes four a trip. Everyone else goes on a raft: free with raft poles or driftwood, otherwise give up any two items. With no raft, they swim, and most highland villagers could not swim. Only Pao and Sheng can.

**river_water** · on screen: current

Third, the water. Each person, children and the sick first, walks the Mekong lane with up to three draws. Down to the water: on three or less they are hit, and on a one they are killed at the water's edge. The current: draw a River card, read it aloud, then draw a Fortune card. Swept away on thirteen or less, plus four in the monsoon, plus two for a child, elder, sick or wounded person; six better in a boat, three better on a raft or at a safe crossing place, and better with floats.

**river_swept** · on screen: river_result

Anyone the current takes draws again: drowned on twelve or less, worse for a non-swimmer without a float, better if someone is already on the Thai bank to throw a line. Above it, they crawl out on the Thai bank. If the river takes someone, you may spend one Hope to draw that check again, once per person. A family that has held on to its spirit has a better chance at the end.

### The end of the game

**end_when** · on screen: scenes/06_across_the_river_1200.jpg

The game ends the moment someone reaches the Thai bank and no one is left in the water, when the last villager dies or is taken, or at the end of Winter 1979. The ending is part of the game. Do not skip it.

**end_cards** · on screen: ending

Read Ending A, Across the Mekong, if anyone crossed, or Ending B, The ones who stayed, if no one did. Say aloud the names of those who did not reach the river. Shuffle the Voice cards of that ending, draw one and read it: each is built from the recollection of a real person. Then, for every villager, find what became of them in the Endings booklet: their name and a number, from one, crossed the Mekong, to ten. Each player names one thing their villager carried the whole way. And read the dedication.

### A season of play

**ex_intro** · on screen: table_full

Let's play a season together, in the app, with Chai, Mai, Chao and Blong. It is Spring 1975 in the Village Ruins. Food is twelve, Hope is three, and four squares of the cloth are sewn.

**ex_season** · on screen: ex_season

The Season card comes first. This one is a dilemma, a Lao family's granary, so the whole party decides before anyone acts. Then the villagers take their actions, Chai first.

**ex_search** · on screen: ex_search

Chai searches the village: one Food, and the top card of the village deck. The item goes to the family.

**ex_search2** · on screen: ex_search2

Chai searches again. This is the second search here, so first a Fortune card: on a one, soldiers would come. We are lucky, and Chai draws another card from the deck. His two actions are spent, so play passes to Mai.

**ex_forage** · on screen: ex_forage

Mai is a farmer. She forages: the village gives its spring Food plus one, for free, and her Fortune card is above three, so no one spots her.

**ex_scout** · on screen: ex_scout

With her second action Mai scouts: one Food, and one of the hidden places in the hills turns face up. Now the party knows where it can go next season.

**ex_rest** · on screen: ex_rest

Chao and Blong finish the season. Chao searches once more, and Blong, the elder, rests. When the last villager is done, the season ends: no one is sick, it is not Winter, so the time marker simply moves to Summer.

**ex_travel** · on screen: ex_travel

Summer 1975. A new Season card, and then, before anyone acts, the family travels to the airstrip Mai scouted. Travel normally costs one Food and a Fortune card against the place's danger number, but Blong, the elder, uses his power, The Old Road: no danger draw this time, and one more Food gathered on the way. The season ends when they arrive. Next season the search begins again, in a new place, with new cards. That is the rhythm of Chao Fa: look, decide, move, and keep the family fed.

### Your first games

**close_ladder** · on screen: ladder

A ladder of first games. Start with The First Winter, the printed first game, or the app's Learn to play, with Chai, Mai, Chao and Blong. Then the same four on the Short Trail, making your own decisions. Then the full Trail with a party of four or five. After that, the villagers with the hardest powers, a setup card, side B, and the Late Crossing scenario.

**close_rules** · on screen: golden

Three golden rules when the table is unsure. A card beats the rulebook. A specific rule beats a general one. And when the table cannot agree, the elders decide: the player of the oldest villager.

**close_end** · on screen: outro

The rulebook, the print-and-play kit and the free app are at the address on screen. Chao Fa is dedicated to all the men and women who served in the Vietnam War, to the Hmong who served and were killed, to those who died in the jungles of Laos and those who are still there. Thank you for watching, and good luck on the trail.
